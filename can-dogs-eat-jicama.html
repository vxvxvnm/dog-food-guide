Can Dogs Eat Jicama? Vet-Reviewed Safety Guide (2025)

Published January 20, 2025 · Reviewed by veterinary nutrition guidelines · 6 min read

✅ Quick Answer

Yes, dogs can safely eat jicama flesh — but only the peeled root. The skin, leaves, and seeds contain rotenone, a compound toxic to dogs. Serve raw, peeled, and in small portions.

What Is Jicama?

Jicama (pronounced "HEE-kah-mah") is a root vegetable native to Mexico and Central America. Also known as Mexican yam bean or Mexican turnip, it has rough brown skin and crisp white flesh that tastes mildly sweet — somewhere between a water chestnut and an apple. It's commonly eaten raw in salads and slaws throughout Latin America and Southeast Asia.

Is Jicama Safe for Dogs?

The short answer: the peeled root is safe, but every other part of the plant is not. The flesh of jicama for dogs can be a hydrating, low-calorie treat — it's roughly 87% water and contains no fat. However, jicama belongs to the legume family (Fabaceae), and the plant's skin, leaves, stems, and seeds produce a natural insecticide called rotenone that protects it from pests.

Rotenone is toxic to mammals — including dogs — when ingested in sufficient amounts. It disrupts cellular respiration and can cause vomiting, drooling, lethargy, muscle tremors, and in severe cases, respiratory failure. This is why knowing which parts dogs eat jicama from matters. Peeled root? Safe. Anything else? Dangerous.

The ASPCA does not list jicama itself as a toxic food, but veterinary toxicology databases consistently flag rotenone exposure from the plant's non-root parts as a concern. So the rule is straightforward: is jicama safe for dogs? Only if you strip the root bare and serve nothing else.

Nutritional Benefits of Jicama for Dogs

Jicama root isn't just a safe filler — it offers several nutritional benefits when fed appropriately:

Calories 38 kcal Ultra-low-calorie treat option
Vitamin C 20 mg Immune health, antioxidant support
Fiber 4.9 g Digestive regularity, prebiotic effect
Potassium 150 mg Muscle function and fluid balance

Jicama is notably rich in inulin, a prebiotic fiber that feeds beneficial gut bacteria. This makes it one of the few crunchy treats that actively supports digestive health rather than just being "neutral" for dogs. The potassium content also supports proper muscle contractions, and the extremely low sugar profile — just 2.7g per 100g — makes it a safer choice for overweight or diabetic dogs compared to sweeter fruits like banana or mango.

Which Parts Can Dogs Eat?

Jicama has more dangerous parts than safe ones. Here's exactly what dogs can and cannot have:

  • ✅ Peeled root flesh — Safe and nutritious. The crunchy white interior is the only part you should feed. Serve raw and unseasoned.
  • ❌ Skin / peel — Toxic. The brown outer skin contains rotenone, a natural pesticide that is harmful to dogs. Always peel completely.
  • ❌ Leaves and stems — Highly toxic. The above-ground parts of the plant contain concentrated rotenone. Keep dogs away from jicama plants in the garden.
  • ❌ Seeds / pods — Highly toxic. The seeds and seed pods contain the highest concentration of rotenone. Never let your dog access them.

How Much Jicama Can Dogs Eat?

Jicama is very low in calories, so portion sizes can be slightly more generous than higher-sugar fruits — but start small and watch your dog's response to the high fiber content:

Small Dogs

Under 20 lbs (9 kg)

1–2 tsp(about 5–10g)

Medium Dogs

20–50 lbs (9–22 kg)

1–2 tbsp(about 15–30g)

Large Dogs

Over 50 lbs (22 kg)

3–4 tbsp(about 45–60g)

Start with half the recommended portion the first time. Jicama's high fiber can cause gas or loose stools if a dog isn't used to it. Wait 24 hours and monitor for any digestive changes before offering more. Stick to 1–2 times per week as a treat — it should never replace balanced meals.

How to Prepare Jicama for Dogs

Jicama preparation is simple, but you must peel it — no shortcuts here:

  1. Select a firm jicama root with no soft spots, sprouts, or mold.
  2. Wash the exterior under running water to remove dirt and surface contaminants.
  3. Using a sharp knife or vegetable peeler, remove every trace of the brown skin. The skin is toxic — don't leave any behind.
  4. Rinse the peeled root once more to wash away any residue.
  5. Cut into matchsticks or small cubes — jicama is firm and large chunks can be a choking hazard.
  6. Serve raw and plain. No salt, lime, chili powder, or dips — those common human toppings can harm dogs.
  7. Optional: freeze matchsticks for a crunchy summer treat that supports dental health through natural chewing.

People Also Ask

Can dogs eat jicama skin?

No. Jicama skin contains rotenone, a natural compound that is toxic to dogs. Ingesting the skin can cause vomiting, drooling, lethargy, and in large amounts, more serious neurological symptoms. Always peel jicama thoroughly before offering any to your dog — and discard the peels where your dog cannot get to them.

Can dogs eat jicama seeds?

No. The seeds, seed pods, leaves, and stems of the jicama plant contain concentrated rotenone, which is highly toxic to dogs. Unlike the root, these above-ground parts are never safe. Keep jicama plants out of reach if you grow them at home, and only feed the peeled root from commercial sources.

Is raw jicama safe for dogs?

Yes — raw jicama root (once fully peeled) is safe and actually the preferred way to serve it. Cooking is unnecessary because jicama contains no harmful compounds in the flesh, and the raw crunch helps scrape plaque from teeth. Cooked jicama is also safe, but it loses its signature crispness and some water-soluble vitamins.

Can puppies eat jicama?

Puppies over 12 weeks old can try a tiny amount — roughly half a teaspoon of peeled, finely diced jicama. Their developing digestive systems are more sensitive to fiber, so introduce it slowly and watch for loose stools. For puppies under 12 weeks, stick exclusively to their regular puppy food and veterinarian-recommended treats.

Final Verdict

So, can dogs eat jicama? Yes — but only the peeled root, and only in moderation. Jicama is one of the few crunchy treats that's both ultra-low in calories (38 kcal per 100g) and actively beneficial, thanks to its prebiotic fiber and vitamin C. The critical safety rule is non-negotiable: remove the skin entirely. The peel, leaves, stems, and seeds all contain rotenone, a natural insecticide that is toxic to dogs. If you follow the rule — peel it, dice it, serve it plain — jicama for dogs is a refreshing, healthy occasional treat. If your dog has a history of pancreatitis, diabetes, or digestive issues, consult your vet before introducing any new food.
