Can Dogs Eat Kimchi? Vet-Reviewed Safety Guide (2025)

Published January 25, 2025 · Reviewed by veterinary nutrition guidelines · 7 min read

❌ Quick Answer

No, dogs should not eat kimchi. It almost always contains garlic, onion, chili, and high levels of sodium — multiple ingredients that are toxic or harmful to dogs. Even a small amount can cause digestive distress.

What Is Kimchi?

Kimchi is a traditional Korean fermented vegetable dish — most commonly made with napa cabbage, radish, gochugaru (Korean chili flakes), garlic, ginger, scallions, fish sauce, and salt. The mixture is fermented for days or weeks, developing a tangy, spicy, deeply savory flavor. It's a staple in Korean cuisine and served as a side dish with nearly every meal.

Is Kimchi Safe for Dogs?

No. In fact, kimchi is one of the worst human foods you can share with your dog. While plain cabbage itself is safe and even healthy for dogs, kimchi brings multiple hazards into one bowl:

Garlic and onion (Allium family): Nearly every kimchi recipe includes garlic, and many include scallions or onion. These allium vegetables contain thiosulphate, a compound that damages canine red blood cells and can cause hemolytic anemia. Garlic is roughly 3–5 times more toxic to dogs than onion, and the effects are cumulative — small amounts over several days add up. A 500g jar of kimchi may contain 10–15g of garlic, which is enough to threaten a 20-lb dog.

Sodium overload: A single 100g serving of commercial kimchi can contain 500–800mg of sodium. For comparison, a 20-lb dog's recommended daily sodium intake is roughly 100–150mg. One bite of kimchi can exceed their daily limit, stressing the kidneys and potentially triggering sodium ion poisoning in sensitive dogs.

Chili (capsaicin): Gochugaru gives kimchi its signature heat, but dogs have far more sensitive digestive tracts than humans. Capsaicin irritates the stomach lining, and even if your dog doesn't show immediate distress, it can cause gastritis and painful diarrhea within hours.

Fermentation byproducts: While fermented foods are sometimes promoted for probiotic benefits, the fermentation process in kimchi also produces histamines and other biogenic amines that can trigger allergic-like reactions in dogs, including itching, swelling, and gastrointestinal upset.

The bottom line: can dogs eat kimchi? No — the risk from multiple toxic and harmful ingredients far outweighs any possible benefit from the cabbage or probiotics.

Which Parts or Types of Kimchi Are Safe?

Let's break down the common variations — but the safe answer is consistent: none are recommended:

  • ❌ Baechu kimchi (napa cabbage) — Not safe. Contains garlic, onion, chili, fish sauce, and high sodium as standard ingredients.
  • ❌ Kkakdugi (radish kimchi) — Not safe. Same seasoning base as cabbage kimchi — garlic, chili, and sodium remain the critical risks.
  • ❌ White kimchi (baek kimchi) — Slightly less dangerous, but still not recommended. It omits chili but retains garlic, ginger, and sodium. Safer? Marginally. Safe? No.
  • ✅ Plain boiled or steamed cabbage — Safe alternative. Plain green or napa cabbage, unseasoned and unfermented, is a healthy, low-calorie treat when fed in moderation.

What to Do If Your Dog Ate Kimchi

Kimchi is unlikely to be fatal from a single small bite, but it can make a dog feel very unwell. Here's how to respond based on how much was eaten:

Symptoms of garlic or onion toxicity may take 24–72 hours to appear. Watch for: lethargy, pale or yellow gums, dark urine, rapid breathing, vomiting, and loss of appetite. These are signs of hemolytic anemia and require immediate veterinary attention.

Safer Alternatives to Kimchi for Dogs

If you want to give your dog a vegetable-based treat with similar crunch or probiotic potential, here are safe swaps:

  1. Plain steamed cabbage — Soft, mild, and rich in fiber. Chop finely and serve 1–2 tablespoons for medium dogs.
  2. Fresh cucumber slices — Crunchy, hydrating, and virtually zero-calorie. No seasoning needed.
  3. Plain sauerkraut (no salt, no garlic) — Only if you can find or make a truly unseasoned version. A half-teaspoon provides natural probiotics.
  4. Dog-specific probiotic supplements — Far safer than fermented human foods. These contain bacteria strains studied specifically for canine gut health.
  5. Steamed green beans — Low-calorie crunch with fiber and vitamins. Most dogs enjoy them plain.

People Also Ask

Can dogs eat kimchi without garlic?

Even if you make kimchi without garlic, it still contains high sodium and often onion, scallions, and chili — all of which can harm dogs. A truly plain, salt-free, allium-free fermented cabbage would technically be low-risk, but at that point it's no longer kimchi. Plain steamed cabbage is a much safer, simpler choice for dogs eat kimchi alternatives.

What should I do if my dog ate kimchi?

First, estimate how much was eaten and check the ingredient list. If the kimchi contained garlic or onion, call your vet or the Pet Poison Helpline (855-764-7661) for guidance. For a tiny lick, monitor for 24 hours and provide plenty of water. For a full bite or more with garlic/onion, seek veterinary care — early treatment with activated charcoal or fluids can make a significant difference.

Can dogs eat fermented vegetables?

Some plain, salt-free fermented vegetables made without garlic or onion can be safe in tiny amounts — a teaspoon or less. However, most commercially fermented foods for humans contain high sodium and seasonings unsuitable for dogs. If you want to add probiotics to your dog's diet, a veterinarian-recommended canine probiotic supplement is far more targeted, safer, and backed by dosing research.

Can dogs eat Korean food?

Most traditional Korean dishes are not dog-safe because they rely heavily on garlic, onion, gochugaru, soy sauce, and fermented pastes — all problematic ingredients. Plain steamed rice and plain grilled meats (marinated in nothing) are generally fine, but the core seasoning ingredients in Korean cuisine make sharing table food risky. Always check individual ingredients before offering any human food to your dog.

Final Verdict

So, can dogs eat kimchi? No — and this is one where the answer is a firm "don't." Unlike some borderline foods where a tiny amount might be fine, kimchi packs multiple hazards into every spoonful: garlic and onion toxicity, excessive sodium, chili irritation, and fermentation byproducts that canine digestive systems aren't adapted to handle. There is no nutritional benefit in kimchi for dogs that can't be obtained more safely from plain vegetables or canine-specific supplements. If your dog snatches a fallen piece off the floor, don't panic — a small lick is unlikely to cause serious harm. But kimchi should never be intentionally fed. Stick to safe, simple vegetables like steamed cabbage or cucumber, and let kimchi remain a human-only food.
