Can Dogs Eat Lychee? Vet-Reviewed Safety Guide (2025)

Published February 5, 2025 · Reviewed by veterinary nutrition guidelines · 6 min read

✅ Quick Answer

Yes, dogs can eat the flesh of ripe, peeled, and deseeded lychee in small amounts. However, the pit and skin are dangerous — the pit contains toxins and is a choking hazard, and the skin is indigestible. Only the white flesh is safe, and only in moderation.

What Is Lychee?

Lychee (Litchi chinensis) is a small tropical fruit native to southern China, now grown across Southeast Asia, India, and parts of the Americas. It has rough reddish-pink skin, translucent white flesh with a floral aroma, and a single glossy brown seed in the center. The flavor is sweet and perfumed — somewhat like a grape crossed with a rose — and it's popular fresh, canned, or in desserts.

Is Lychee Safe for Dogs?

The flesh of a ripe lychee is safe for dogs — but that sentence requires three qualifiers: ripe, flesh, and in moderation. Remove any one of those, and the safety picture changes sharply.

The ripe flesh is fine. Fully ripened lychee pulp contains no compounds known to be directly toxic to dogs. It's mostly water (82%), with natural sugars (15g per 100g) and a solid dose of vitamin C. In small amounts — a few peeled, pitted fruits — lychee for dogs makes a sweet, hydrating summer snack.

The pit is dangerous in two ways. First, it's a physical choking hazard — lychee pits are smooth, round, and roughly the diameter of a grape, making them a perfect size to lodge in a dog's throat. Second, the pit contains hypoglycin A and methylene cyclopropyl glycine (MCPG), the same metabolic toxins found in ackee fruit. These compounds disrupt glucose metabolism and can cause a sudden, severe drop in blood sugar — acute hypoglycemia — especially in smaller dogs. This is the same toxin associated with illness outbreaks from unripe lychee consumption in children.

The skin is an obstruction risk. The rough, leathery peel isn't toxic in the chemical sense, but it's completely indigestible and can form a stubborn intestinal blockage, especially in small dogs. Pesticide residues are an additional concern.

Unripe lychee is toxic. Green or under-ripe lychee contains significantly higher concentrations of hypoglycin A — far more than fully ripe fruit. If you're going to offer lychee, it must be fully ripe: red or pink skin that yields slightly to pressure.

The core question: can dogs eat lychee? Yes — a small amount of ripe, peeled, pitted flesh is safe. But the gap between "safe" and "dangerous" is narrower than with many other fruits, so preparation really matters.

Nutritional Profile of Lychee

Lychee is nutrient-dense for its size, but its sugar content means it's best as an occasional treat:

Nutrient Per 100g Benefit or Risk for Dogs
Calories 66 kcal Moderate — reasonable for a treat
Vitamin C 71.5 mg Strong antioxidant boost; 80%+ of human RDA per 100g
Sugar 15.2 g High sugar — limit intake strictly
Fiber 1.3 g Mild digestive support
Copper 0.15 mg Supports red blood cell formation
Potassium 171 mg Fluid balance and nerve function
Water content ~82% Hydrating summer treat

Lychee's standout nutrient is vitamin C — at 71.5mg per 100g, it outstrips most fruits, including oranges. While dogs synthesize their own vitamin C, supplementary dietary sources provide additional antioxidant protection, particularly in active, aging, or stressed dogs. The trade-off is the high sugar content, which is why portion control is far more important with lychee than with lower-sugar fruits like dragon fruit or cucumber.

Which Parts Can Dogs Eat?

Lychee has a strict "one part safe, everything else dangerous" profile. Here's the breakdown:

  • ✅ Ripe flesh / pulp — Safe in moderation. The translucent white aril is the only part a dog should eat. Must be fully ripe (red skin, not green).
  • ❌ Seed / pit — Highly dangerous. Choking hazard plus metabolic toxins (hypoglycin A). A single pit can cause hypoglycemia in small dogs. Remove and discard entirely.
  • ❌ Skin / peel — Dangerous. Indigestible, rough texture that can cause mechanical intestinal blockage. May carry pesticide residues.
  • ❌ Unripe (green) lychee — Toxic. Contains up to 10x the hypoglycin A of ripe fruit. Can cause severe hypoglycemia and vomiting. Never feed.

How Much Lychee Can Dogs Eat?

Lychee portions are smaller than for lower-sugar fruits because of the concentrated sugar and the risk profile:

Small Dogs

Under 20 lbs (9 kg)

½ lychee(about 5g flesh)

Medium Dogs

20–50 lbs (9–22 kg)

1 lychee(about 10g flesh)

Large Dogs

Over 50 lbs (22 kg)

2 lychees(about 20g flesh)

Offer lychee no more than once a week. The high sugar content (15g per 100g) makes it a special treat rather than a regular snack. For dogs with diabetes, obesity, or pancreatitis, skip lychee entirely — the sugar content and fruit acids are not appropriate for dogs with metabolic conditions.

How to Prepare Lychee for Dogs

Lychee requires more careful prep than most fruits. Don't shortcut these steps:

  1. Choose ripe lychees — red or pink skin, slightly yielding to pressure. Avoid green, brown, or rock-hard fruits.
  2. Wash the exterior thoroughly to remove dirt and pesticide residues.
  3. Peel the skin completely with your fingers or a knife. Discard the peel where your dog can't access it.
  4. Cut the flesh away from the seed — the flesh clings to the pit, so you'll need to slice it off. Do not simply pop the seed out and hand the rest to your dog.
  5. Discard the pit in a sealed trash container. Dogs have been known to scavenge discarded pits.
  6. Chop the flesh into small, bite-sized pieces appropriate for your dog's size.
  7. Serve plain at room temperature. Never offer canned lychee — it's packed in syrup with up to 20g of added sugar per serving.

People Also Ask

Can dogs eat lychee seeds?

No — lychee seeds (pits) must be completely removed and safely discarded. They pose two threats: a physical choking and obstruction risk due to their smooth, round shape, and a metabolic toxin risk from hypoglycin A and MCPG, which can trigger a dangerous drop in blood sugar. Even large dogs should never be given lychee with the pit intact. Always cut the flesh off the pit — don't simply pop the pit out and hand the fruit over.

Can dogs eat lychee skin?

No. Lychee skin is tough, leathery, and indigestible. If swallowed, it can form an intestinal blockage — especially in small dogs — that may require surgical removal. The rough surface also traps pesticides and environmental contaminants. Always peel lychee completely and dispose of the skins in a secure trash bin that your dog cannot access.

Can unripe lychee hurt dogs?

Yes — unripe (green) lychee is significantly more dangerous than ripe lychee. It contains up to 10 times more hypoglycin A, the metabolic toxin linked to acute hypoglycemia. Both the flesh and the pit of unripe lychee are hazardous. If you're not confident that a lychee is fully ripe (red-pink skin, sweet aroma, slightly soft to touch), do not feed it to your dog. The risk is not worth it.

Can puppies eat lychee?

Puppies over 12 weeks old can have a tiny taste — about a quarter of a peeled, deseeded lychee, mashed. Because puppies have lower body mass and less stable blood sugar regulation, they are more vulnerable to the hypoglycemic effects of any residual pit toxins and to the sugar spike from the fruit itself. Start with an amount smaller than your fingernail and wait 24 hours. For puppies under 12 weeks, stick to their regular food only.

Final Verdict

So, is lychee safe for dogs? Yes — but only the ripe, peeled, deseeded flesh, and only in strict moderation. The rules are non-negotiable: ripe fruit only (red, never green), peel removed, pit removed and discarded securely, and portions limited to 1–2 fruits max depending on body weight. The toxic pit and indigestible skin make lychee one of the higher-maintenance fruits to prepare for dogs, and the high sugar content means it's a once-a-week treat at most. If you're looking for a safer tropical fruit with less prep hassle, dogs eat lychee alternatives like dragon fruit or mango are simpler and lower-risk. But if you're willing to do the careful prep, a small amount of ripe lychee flesh is a sweet, antioxidant-rich occasional snack your dog can safely enjoy. If your dog accidentally eats a whole lychee — pit, skin, or unripe fruit — contact your veterinarian immediately.
